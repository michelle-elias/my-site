About

Background in physiologically based pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic (PBPK/PD) modelling, digital twins of drugs and reproducibility studies of published models. Current work moves from the whole body to the tissue scale: AI-based segmentation of whole-slide liver histology, with the resulting feature maps informing spatial PDE–ODE simulations of transport and tissue damage.

Main interests: mechanistic modelling, biomedical image analysis, and open, reproducible research software.

  • Master project

    08/2026 – 01/2027

    Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin

    Data and image-analysis foundation: metadata schema, scalable storage of whole-slide images (OME-TIFF/OME-Zarr), AI-assisted necrosis segmentation, quantitative feature maps and interactive 2D visualization.

  • Master thesis

    02/2027 – 09/2027

    Universität zu Lübeck

    Registration of neighbouring tissue sections, image-to-mesh mapping and coupled PDE–ODE simulation (FEniCSx, libRoadRunner), with simulation results returned to the viewer as time-dependent layers. Use cases: APAP-induced liver necrosis and hepatic steatosis.

Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Matthias König

Bachelor thesis · B.Sc. Biology

A physiological-based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model of the sulfonylurea glimepiride

2025

Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · König Lab

Whole-body PBPK model of the antidiabetic drug glimepiride in SBML, covering renal and hepatic impairment, bodyweight and CYP2C9 genotype, validated against 20 clinical studies. Basis of the published glimepiride digital twin and its interactive web app.

Projects

  • SlideViz

    2026 · in development

    Viewer and data layer for whole-slide liver histology: slides with JSON metadata, a queryable slide index and multiscale display in napari. Basis for segmentation results and registered layers in the master project.

    GitHub

  • VisFEM

    2026

    Web platform for interactive visualization of finite-element (FEM) simulation results in biomedical research, developed within DFG SPP 2311. Open source and installable from PyPI; manuscript in preparation.

    GitHub PyPI DOI

  • Polysome resolution & translation prediction

    2025 – 2026 · course project

    CNN-based prediction of mean ribosome load from 5′ UTR sequences, testing how reduced polysome-profiling resolution affects model performance. 280,000 sequences, 5-fold cross-validation across five resolution schemes; interpretation via Integrated Gradients (Captum).

    GitHub
